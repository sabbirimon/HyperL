# HyperL licensing guide and legal review record

[LICENSE](../LICENSE) controls; this guide does not expand rights.
Licensing contact: **sabbirimon@gmail.com**.

| Newly covered use | Fee / duration |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | Free, no six-month expiry |
| Developers, including commercial app development and enterprise employees | Free non-production development/testing |
| Academic, educational, scientific and non-production industrial research | Free, no size limit |
| Small-business production | Free while below both group thresholds |
| Large-entity production | One six-calendar-month trial, then paid written agreement or stop that production use |

A large group has **at least US$10 million consolidated annual gross revenue or
approved annual operating budget, or 100 employees**. Small businesses must be
below both. Affiliates count together; unrelated customers/suppliers do not.
Government/nonprofit budget classification covers operational deployments;
genuine research remains free.

Examples: a developer selling an app can develop it freely. A 40-person business
with US$2 million revenue can run newly covered HyperL freely. A 120-person business
qualifies even with US$2 million revenue. University experiments remain free;
a large university's production administration falls under the enterprise rule.
A cloud operator evaluates its own deployment; merely receiving results does not
impose a licence on an end user. Contractor accounts do not exempt an enterprise's
own deployment.

The trial begins at first qualifying production use of newly covered material.
It is six **calendar months**, not accumulated runtime. A trial starting
8 October 2026 ends at the start of 8 April 2027. For 31 August, it ends at the
start of February's final day if that numbered date does not exist. Upgrades,
pauses, reinstalls and new subsidiaries do not reset it. Growth across a threshold
starts a trial then; eligible past small-business use is not charged retroactively.

Ask for terms before expiry. No price, billing account, automatic charge, SLA,
indemnity or support promise is created here. A negotiated agreement must name
legal parties, covered groups/deployments, version/update rights, term, price/
currency/tax/payment, support/security commitments if any, remedies and governing
law/forum agreed by those parties. This repository does not invent a jurisdiction
or sell unavailable services. No telemetry or remote-disable mechanism is added.

## Transition and limits

[History](LICENSE_HISTORY.md) preserves the Apache tree through `67b30e0` and
released alpha.5 assets. Earlier Apache enterprise/fork rights remain. A notice
change cannot make identical old code exclusively restricted. Fees can apply to
newly covered rights, not the old Apache code retroactively.

The custom licence is **source available**, not OSI-approved open source:
enterprise use is restricted. See the [Apache copyright grant](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.html)
and [OSI definition](https://opensource.org/osd). Dependencies, models, fonts,
vendor SDKs and independent outputs retain separate rights. The prior Meshlit port
is unaffected.

## Maintainer checks

- Preserve authorship, upstream notices and historical Apache text.
- Review transitive/bundled runtime redistribution, including OpenJDK source or
  source-offer obligations, before releasing installers.
- Obtain actual contributor authority before commercial sublicensing. The
  contributor agreement is a template, not evidence of acceptance.
- Ship LICENSE/NOTICE/history with portable/native and Python packages; never
  relabel or overwrite alpha.5 artifacts as an alpha.6 release.
- Do not claim a payment clause, branch ruleset or code audit proves patent
  clearance, every ownership right, production safety or legal enforceability.

These custom terms reflect the owner's instructions. Qualified legal review is
still required before commercial enforcement: applicable copyright/contract law,
thresholds, trial remedies, contributor authority, consumer rights, commercial
agreements and cross-border enforceability. No automated audit establishes
freedom from every legal issue or third-party patent.
